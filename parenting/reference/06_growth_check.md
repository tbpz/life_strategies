# Growth Check — 14 to 36 Months

> Read this file at each monthly weigh-in, or when unsure whether the son's eating is still a normal phase.
> Growth is the test. While weight and height stay inside the ranges below and follow the son's own line, the eating phase is treated as normal (`DECISIONS.md`, 2026-10-24, eating phase). When a warning sign below appears, the phase is no longer treated as normal.

---

## How to weigh and measure

- Once a month, on the same day of the month.
- Same scale each time, in the morning before food, in a dry diaper or light clothes.
- Height: lying down up to 23 months, standing from 24 months. Standing reads about 0.7 cm shorter, which explains the small step in the height table at 24 months. Measure at the clinic if possible; a home tape is easy to get wrong by 1–2 cm.
- Write the number in the **Measured** column of the tables below.

## How to read the numbers

- At this age a child gains only about 150–250 g a month. One flat month, or one reading 200 g lower, means nothing: clothes, a full stomach or a different scale can cause that.
- Look at the direction over 2–3 checks, not at one month.
- **Low end and High end** are the range WHO counts as healthy (2 steps below and above the average on WHO's own scale, roughly the 2nd to 98th percentile). Most boys fall inside it.
- **The son's line** is where the son would be each month if the son stays at the same place on the chart as at 14 months (about 11.0 kg, roughly the 78th percentile). Following that line is the best sign. Moving away from it matters more than being inside the range.

## Warning signs — stop treating the eating as a normal phase

### Go to a doctor or hospital the same day

- No wet diaper for 8 hours or more, no tears when crying, sunken eyes, or unusually sleepy and hard to wake (signs of drying out).
- Blood in vomit or stool.
- Trouble breathing, or a blue look around the mouth, after choking or coughing on food.
- Loses a skill already learned, such as walking or words.

### Book a doctor visit this week

**Growth**
- Weight below the **Low end** at any check.
- Weight lower than the month before at two checks in a row.
- Weight below the **Average** column at two checks in a row. The son started well above it, so falling that far means a real move away from the son's own line.
- No weight gain for 3 months.
- Height stops growing for 3 months.

**Body**
- Looks pale, tires easily, or is cranky for no clear reason. Possible low iron, even with the supplement.
- Eats dirt, paper or other things that are not food.
- Vomits often, has diarrhoea for more than a week, or has hard, painful stools.

**Eating**
- Refuses even the usual safe foods (carrot, chayote, gourd, noodles) for more than a few days.
- Lives almost only on milk.
- Gags, coughs or chokes at most meals, or cannot handle any lumps at all.
- Screams or panics at the sight of food. This is more than ordinary refusing.

**What to ask for at that visit:** a proper check of the cause, including the blood test the hospital doctor skipped before (iron level and blood count). The first doctor judged the son fine on growth. That changes once growth stops following the line.

---

## When the monthly check can stop

At 3 years, if weight and height have followed the son's line or stayed inside the range, return to the usual yearly checks. The eating phase is then handled as ordinary picky eating, with no more monthly weigh-ins needed.
