# Kindergarten Plan

**Child's age now:** 13 months (Oct 2026) | **Target start:** Aug 2027 (~23 months) | **Latest start:** 3 years

---

## Target: August 2027

- **Still before age 2.** This fits the Stage 2 goal of regular time with other kids before 2.
- **Start of the school year.** The child joins a new class with other new kids, not mid-year. Teachers expect new kids to need time to settle at this point.
- **Separation anxiety has mostly passed.** It peaks around 15–18 months, so drop-offs are easier at ~23 months.
- **The nanny covers the hardest months.** Tet and the hot season are spent at home, where care is steadier.

Slipping to ~2.5 years is fine, for example if the preferred school has no place until early 2028. Starting much later than 3 is not.

---

## When school is needed

| Age | Need for school | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Under 2 | Not needed | One steady, familiar adult matters most. A good nanny does this best. |
| 2–3 | Helpful, not needed | Kids start playing *with* each other, not just side by side. School helps; playgroups and outings can cover it. |
| 3+ | Needed | Other kids are needed to practise sharing, waiting a turn, handling arguments and following group routines. Home can't give this. |
| 5 | Expected | The official kindergarten year before grade 1 at age 6. |

**Signs the child is outgrowing home, at any age:** bored at home, runs toward other kids to join in, the nanny can't keep up with the energy, talks in short sentences, copes with a few hours apart.

## Worries, checked

| Worry | Valid? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Gets sick a lot | **Yes.** Many more colds, stomach bugs and ear infections in the first year. Mostly these come earlier rather than adding extra; late starters catch them in primary school instead. | Don't start under 18 months. Keep the nanny as backup for sick days during the first months. |
| Can't tell parents what happened | **Yes, the most important one.** Abuse at small private kindergartens has made the news in Vietnam. At ~2 the child has a few words; by 2.5–3, short sentences. | Choose the school carefully (see questions and visit checks below). Watch for changes at home after starting. |
| Can't eat alone yet | **Weak.** Teachers feed or help kids at this age. School is often where kids learn, by copying others. | Ask how meals work. Practise spoon and cup at home now. |
| Early starters are "more energetic" | **Mostly misleading.** Energy level is mostly inborn. Early starters can be more confident with other kids, but also slightly more defiant. Late starters catch up within months. | Regular outings now give most of the benefit, without school. |

## Questions to ask each school

*Not yet confirmed. Rules vary by school.*

**Places and dates**

- Youngest age accepted?
- When do new kids start (only Aug/Sep, or any month)?
- How long is the waiting list?
- Public schools: are kids grouped by birth year? Is a late-2025 birth accepted for the 2027–28 school year?
- When does registration open for a September start?

**Daily care** (a school can answer these directly; for the first two, also check by eye, see below)

- How many kids per teacher in the class for this age? (5 or fewer is good; more than 8 is a warning.)
- Are the teachers the same every day, or do staff rotate?
- Can parents watch the cameras? Can they see it live on a phone?
- Can parents drop in without warning?
- What are the written rules for sick kids (when they must stay home)?
- How do meals work for this age? Who feeds slow eaters?

## What to watch during visits

*A school will say "yes, of course" if asked. These can only be judged by seeing them.*

- [ ] Crying kids are picked up and comforted, not ignored or put in front of a TV
- [ ] Slow eaters are helped and get enough food
- [ ] Clean rooms, toilets and kitchen (ask to see the toilets and the kitchen)
- [ ] Count the kids and teachers in the room. Does it match the answer given?
- [ ] See the camera view working, not just hear that cameras exist

**Tip:** Visit around mealtime or drop-off (about 7–8am), when crying and eating can be seen. A visit with no warning shows more than a planned tour.

---

## After starting: watch for

- Sleep problems that don't settle after the first few weeks
- Suddenly scared to go, or scared of a specific teacher
- Unexplained marks or bruises
- Big changes in behaviour or mood

Any of these → visit without warning, check the cameras, and talk to the school.
